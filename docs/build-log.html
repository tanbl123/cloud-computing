1. How to use this log

All three of us have to build the same architecture in our own lab accounts. That only works if we agree on the values before anyone starts clicking, because if one person uses 10.0.0.0/16 and another uses 172.31.0.0/16, our screenshots stop matching each other and the report becomes impossible to write consistently.

So this log has one job: to be the single source of truth for what we build. Section 2 holds the agreed values. Nobody changes a value in Section 2 on their own. If you think a value is wrong, put it in the Decision Log (Section 5) and tell the group, then change it once we agree.

Section 3 is the build procedure. Work through it in order. When you finish a step, put your initials and the date in the progress tracker (Section 4) and capture the evidence that step lists, because the report is marked on evidence and screenshots are much harder to recreate later than to take at the time.

Section 6 is for problems. Log them even after you fix them. If one of us hits an error, the other two will probably hit the same one, and a solved problem written down saves the next person an hour.

5. Decision log

Anything we chose deliberately goes here, with the reason. This section feeds directly into the design rationale and cost sections of the report, so writing it down now saves inventing justifications later.

IDDateDecisionReasonDecided by
D-01[ ]App instances in public or private subnetsPrivate subnets are the better design, but they need a NAT gateway for outbound access, which costs money hourly whether or not it is used. If the budget will not carry it, the alternative is public subnets with the app security group restricted to the ALB only.[ ]
D-02[ ]ASG minimum of 2One instance cannot survive an instance or AZ failure, so 2 is the smallest resilient size rather than spare capacity.[ ]
D-03[ ]Single-AZ databasePermitted by the assignment scope, and Multi-AZ roughly doubles the database cost for a standby that serves no traffic. Recorded as a known limitation in the report.[ ]
